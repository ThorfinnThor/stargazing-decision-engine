# Editorial writing standard

This document is the binding writing standard for all destination, location-tour, gear, and guide copy published by Stargazing Index. It applies to new work and to revisions of existing pages.

## The page must answer its own search intent

Treat every page as an independent editorial assignment. Its opening, order, headings, length, examples, practical details, and ending must follow the reader's actual question. Do not impose a shared article template. A comparison, field plan, technical explanation, access decision, or troubleshooting guide may each need a different shape.

The first paragraphs must show that the page understands the specific problem. Put decision-critical facts early enough that a reader can answer the immediate questions without searching through an essay. Longer context belongs after the practical answer.

## Voice and construction

Write naturally, directly, and professionally. Explain a subject as a knowledgeable person would explain it to an interested reader. Concrete information is more useful than broad claims. Remove paragraphs that repeat the obvious or add length without adding understanding.

Avoid repeated openings, sentence rhythms, transitions, heading sequences, and closing sections across pages. In particular, do not use stock AI transitions or command-like formulas such as:

- “First understand what you need. Then compare products.”
- “First you should consider …”
- “In the next step …”
- “But before we get to that …”
- “It is important to note …”
- “In today's world …”
- “Ultimately, it comes down to …”
- “In summary …”
- “Zuerst solltest du dir überlegen …”
- “Im nächsten Schritt …”
- “Doch bevor wir dazu kommen …”
- “Es ist wichtig zu beachten …”
- “In der heutigen Zeit …”
- “Letztendlich kommt es darauf an …”
- “Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen …”

Prefer complete, connected sentences over artificial sequences of short instructions. In ordinary prose, use colons and dashes sparingly. Lists are appropriate when they materially improve scanning, not as a default page-building device.

## Structure, SEO, and evidence

Do not repeat a standard sequence such as introduction, advantages, disadvantages, buying advice, FAQ, and conclusion unless that sequence genuinely fits the subject. A conclusion is optional and must add a decision or implication rather than restate the article.

Use search terms, synonyms, and technical vocabulary where they belong naturally. Never force keywords or repeat them for ranking. Cover the information a reader would still need after the initial answer so that another page is not required merely to complete the decision.

Never invent facts, specifications, test results, personal experience, numbers, or access claims. Existing facts may be clarified and placed in context, but a claim must remain traceable to its cited source. Prefer official public bodies, protected-area operators, science institutions, and other open-access primary sources. Do not copy source prose.

## Review requirement

Review the whole page rather than polishing isolated sentences. Check information order, missing questions, redundant passages, heading quality, paragraph rhythm, factual support, and similarity to other pages. The finished page should read as if a subject-matter author wrote it specifically for that topic, not as one output in an automated series.
